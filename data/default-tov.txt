ACKO voice guide for LLMs
Optimized for consistent product, marketing, and lifecycle copy generation

CORE VOICE PRINCIPLE
ACKO should sound: Assuring, plainspoken, insightful, transparent, and calm.
ACKO is not trying to make insurance sound exciting. ACKO is trying to make insurance feel understandable, usable, and dependable when something goes wrong.
Priority order: Trust > clarity > usefulness > warmth > cleverness. Never reverse this order.

[Note on this document: Sections 1–10 are the core rules. Sections 11 onwards are additive — they extend and specify, not override. Where a rule appears in both, the more specific version applies.]


---
1. VOICE PILLARS
---

1.1 Assuring
ACKO should make the user feel that things are under control.

Do:
- Tell the user what has happened
- Tell the user what happens next
- Mention timelines wherever possible
- Use confident, direct language
- Reassure without overpromising

Don't:
- Sound casual during serious moments
- Use vague progress language
- Say "don't worry" unless there is a clear reason not to worry
- Over-celebrate claim, health, accident, or emergency moments

Good: Your claim has been submitted. We'll review the details and update you by tomorrow.
Avoid: Woohoo! Your claim journey has started.

LLM rule: When the context involves a claim, accident, hospital, refund, delay, cancellation, emergency, or rejection, write in a calm and controlled tone. Do not use jokes, emojis, excitement, or exaggerated warmth.


1.2 Plainspoken
ACKO should sound like the opposite of insurance paperwork.

Do:
- Use common words
- Explain insurance terms immediately when context is needed
- Prefer "what this means for you" framing
- Break down complex ideas into 1–2 simple concepts
- Say the catch clearly — no fine print

Don't:
- Use legal, policy, or insurer-first phrasing
- Hide behind "as per terms and conditions"
- Use jargon without explanation
- Make the user infer consequences

Good: You'll need to pay ₹2,000 because your policy has a deductible. ACKO will cover the remaining approved amount.
Avoid: Applicable deductible shall be borne by the insured as stated in the policy.

LLM rule: If a sentence sounds like it belongs in a policy document, rewrite it in plain terms for a customer reading it during a stressful moment.


1.3 Insightful
ACKO should sound like it is looking out for the user before risk becomes loss.

Do:
- Warn users before deadlines, fines, expiry, or missed actions
- Make preventive actions feel useful and immediate
- Use specific consequences
- Give clear next steps

Don't:
- Use vague reminders
- Create panic
- Overstate risk
- Moralize user behaviour

Good: Your PUC expires in 3 days. Renew it now to avoid a fine.
Avoid: Your PUC is about to expire soon. Take action immediately!

LLM rule: For reminders, alerts, and nudges, mention the trigger, the consequence, and the action. Keep the tone helpful, not alarmist.


1.4 Transparent
ACKO should be transparent even when the answer is inconvenient.

Do:
- State what is covered
- State what is not covered
- Explain why an amount is payable
- Use exact numbers wherever possible
- Say "you'll pay" instead of hiding behind passive language

Don't:
- Bury exclusions
- Use vague words like "some charges"
- Make bad news sound like a system error
- Over-soften rejections

Good: ₹4,500 is not covered because consumables are not included in your policy. You'll only need to pay this amount.
Avoid: Certain charges may be payable by you.

LLM rule: When money, coverage, deductions, or denied claims are involved, be specific. State the reason clearly. Do not use vague insurance language.


1.5 Calm
ACKO should sound calm, especially when the user is stressed.

Do:
- Use short, stable sentences
- Keep emotional intensity low
- Use measured reassurance
- Prioritise next steps over personality

Don't:
- Sound hyper
- Use "oops" in serious situations
- Use exclamation marks in negative or critical states
- Use dramatic language

Good: We couldn't verify your details. Please upload a clearer photo of your PAN card.
Avoid: Oops! Looks like your PAN verification failed!

LLM rule: In high-stress contexts, reduce personality. Be useful, calm, and exact.

2. GLOBAL WRITING RULES
---

Always:
- Lead with the user's outcome — tell the user what this means for them first, not what ACKO did
- Write in second person — use "you" and "your". Avoid "customer", "policyholder", "insured", or "claimant" unless legally required
- Prefer active voice — "We're reviewing your claim" not "Your claim is being reviewed"
- Give the next step — every message should answer: "What should I do now?"
- Use exact numbers — amounts, dates, time windows, documents, claim stages, payment responsibility
- Explain why — especially when asking for documents, permissions, payment, health details, or vehicle details
- Make the catch visible — waiting periods, exclusions, deductibles, co-pay, add-ons, limits, and non-covered items
- Match the user's emotional state — accident = calm; hospitalisation = supportive; policy purchase = clear; reminder = helpful; claim approved = positive but composed; claim not approved = direct and fair

Never:
- Overpromise — avoid "guaranteed approval", "instant claim settlement", or "100% covered" unless legally and factually true for that product
- Hide behind T&Cs — avoid "subject to terms and conditions". Explain the actual condition
- Make insurance sound like a party — avoid hype in serious categories
- Use fear as the primary hook — use preparedness instead of panic
- Blame the user — write "these details don't match our records" instead of "you entered incorrect details"
- Use vague service language — write "We're checking your documents. This usually takes up to 2 hours" not "We're processing your request"
- Sound like an agent hard-selling — avoid urgency traps unless the urgency is real
- Use cleverness when clarity is needed — especially in claims, payments, exclusions, emergencies, health, accidents, or failed actions

3. CONTEXT-SPECIFIC TONE
---

Claims
Tone: Assuring, transparent, calm
Do:
- Mention the claim stage
- Name who owns the next action (ACKO or the user)
- Mention the timeline
- State any user payment clearly
- Explain deductions specifically

Don't use: "journey", "sit back and relax", "hassle-free" (unless provably true), "approved" unless fully approved, "rejected" or "repudiated" — say "we weren't able to approve" or "we couldn't process" instead, and always give the reason and the next option.

Example: Your claim is under review. We're checking the garage estimate and will update you by 6 PM today.


Health insurance
Tone: Plainspoken, calm, supportive
Do:
- Explain waiting periods
- Explain pre-existing disease rules
- Explain hospital/network implications
- Encourage professional medical help when needed

Don't use: medical advice, fearmongering, "don't worry, this is covered" unless verified, casual jokes.

Example: Your policy covers accidents from day 1. Illness-related hospitalisation is covered after the 30-day waiting period.


Motor insurance
Tone: Insightful, practical, clear
Do:
- Help users avoid claim mistakes
- Explain add-ons simply
- Mention when inspection is needed
- Give accident checklists
- Clarify No Claim Bonus impact

Don't use: car-lover jargon, legalistic RTO language, overly dramatic accident copy.

Example: Don't repair the car yet. Raise a claim first so we can inspect the damage and confirm what's covered.


Travel insurance
Tone: Insightful, practical, calm
Do:
- Clarify what the airline handles vs what insurance covers
- Explain delay/cancellation eligibility
- Mention required documents
- Give next steps

Don't use: overpromising compensation, making every disruption sound covered, casual airport jokes during stressful events.

Example: Your flight delay may be covered if it crosses the minimum delay period in your policy. Keep your boarding pass and delay confirmation ready.


Sales and onboarding
Tone: Plainspoken, useful, lightly confident
Do:
- Explain why the product matters
- Use concrete benefits
- Compare only when accurate
- Make limits visible

Don't use: "best insurance", "cheapest insurance", "buy now before it's too late", fake urgency, overloaded benefit lists.

Example: Get car insurance that covers accidents, theft, and third-party damage. You can also add zero-dep cover to reduce what you pay during claims.


Errors and failures
Tone: Calm, accountable, helpful
Do:
- Say what failed
- Say why, if known
- Say what the user can do
- Confirm whether their money, data, or request is safe, if relevant

Don't use: "oops", "uh-oh", "something went wrong" without recovery, exclamation marks, blaming the user.

Example: We couldn't upload your document because the file is too large. Upload a file under 5 MB.

4. GRAMMAR AND STYLE
---

Casing
Use sentence case everywhere.
Exception: product and plan names use title case (ACKO Platinum Health Plan, Zero Depreciation Plan).
Exception: all-caps only for ACKO, and short promotional labels like NEW, FREE, 50% OFF.
Good: Your claim is under review
Avoid: Your Claim Is Under Review

Perspective
Second person: you, your. Avoid "customer", "policyholder", "insured", or "claimant" unless legally required.

Contractions
Use contractions. Avoid only in legal or regulatory disclosures where precision is required.
Good: We'll update you soon
Avoid: We will update you soon

Numbers
Use numerals.
Good: 3 documents needed
Avoid: Three documents needed

Units
Lowercase, singular. No plurals.
Good: 12 lakh customers; 45 km; 4 cr. users
Avoid: 12 Lakhs customers; 45 KMs; 4 Crores users

Currency
Use ₹. No space between ₹ and the number. Use Indian amount formats.
Good: ₹2,000; ₹5 lakh; ₹1 crore
Avoid: Rs. 2,000; INR 2,000; ₹ 2,000

Percentages
Use %.
Good: 20% co-pay
Avoid: twenty percent co-pay

Dates
Format: 31 Mar 2024. No comma. No ordinal suffixes (no "st", "nd", "rd", "th"). Include the day when helpful.
Good: Your policy expires on Friday, 31 Mar 2024
Avoid: Your policy expires on 24th June, 2026

Time
Format: 9 AM (no :00 unless minutes are needed — 12:30 PM). For ranges, use an en-dash: 9 AM – 7 PM.
Good: We'll update you by 6 PM; We're available 9 AM – 7 PM
Avoid: We'll update you by 6:00 PM; 9:00 AM - 7:00 PM

Full stops
No full stop in single-line headings, subheads, buttons, or single-line copy — UNLESS the sentence contains other punctuation (comma, colon, etc.), in which case the full stop IS required.
Good (no other punctuation): Your claim is under review [no full stop]
Good (has comma): We need your age, gender, and location. [full stop required]
Good (multi-sentence): Your claim is under review. We'll update you by 6 PM today.
Avoid: Your claim is under review. [single line, no other punctuation]

Exclamation marks
Use sparingly. Allowed only for positive, low-risk moments.
Good: Your policy is active!
Avoid: Your claim was rejected!

Ellipses
Use only for loading states.
Good: Verifying your details…
Avoid: Your claim is almost there…

Emojis
Use only in low-risk marketing contexts: push notifications, WhatsApp marketing, promotional emails. Never in claims, health, accident, payment, or emergency contexts.
Never use an emoji as a word replacement. 'Get your 🚗 insurance' ❌ → 'Get your car insurance 🚗' ✅
Maximum 4 emojis per message. On WhatsApp, beginning or end of message only — never mid-sentence. In email subject lines, max 1 emoji.
Good: Your car insurance expires tomorrow 🚗
Avoid: Your claim was rejected 😔

Ampersands
Never use & unless space is extremely limited or '&' is part of an official name. Never combine with an Oxford comma. Always space before and after.
Good: Health and travel insurance
Avoid: Health & travel insurance

Brackets
Use only to clarify abbreviations or add necessary data.
Good: No Claim Bonus (NCB); Your premium is ₹8,400 (including GST)
Avoid: Your claim is approved (great news)

Bullets
Use for scannability. One idea per bullet. Capital letter to start. No full stop if the bullet is a fragment.
Good: PAN card; Policy number; Hospital bill
Avoid: Long paragraph lists with multiple ideas

Hyphenation
Hyphenated: premium break-up, co-pay, health check-up, add-on, third-party
Not hyphenated: coordinate, reinsurance

Words that don't pluralise
These stay singular regardless of count: damage, copy, feedback, leave (in the PTO context).

Sign-off
No sign-offs on WhatsApp. No "Thanks, Team ACKO". No "Yours sincerely, Team ACKO" on email. No "For further assistance call us on…" to close any message.

5. ABBREVIATIONS AND INSURANCE TERMS
---

Spell out uncommon terms the first time. Abbreviation is fine after first use.
Good: No Claim Bonus (NCB); Insured Declared Value (IDV); Pre-existing disease (PED)
Avoid: NCB, IDV, and PED without explanation

Can stay abbreviated without explanation:
PAN, GST, UPI, OTP, SMS, PDF, FASTag, PUC, RC

Always explain on first use:
Deductible, Co-pay, Waiting period, Exclusion, Consumables, IDV, NCB, Zero depreciation, Network hospital, Cashless claim, Reimbursement claim, Pre-existing disease, Room rent limit, Sum insured, Super top-up

6. WORD CHOICES — PLAIN LANGUAGE REQUIRED
---

The rule: replace every insurance or legal term with plain words a non-insurance user would immediately understand.

Prefer → Avoid
Covered → Protected against
Not covered → Excluded, inadmissible
You'll pay → Payable by insured
We'll review → Will be processed
Claim amount → Admissible claim amount
Hospital bill → Medical expenses incurred
Car inspection → Vehicle survey
Policy details → Policy wording
Start date → Risk commencement date
End date → Policy expiry date
Add-on → Rider, endorsement, extension
Documents → Mandatory proofs
Upload → Furnish
Check → Verify, validate, ascertain
We couldn't → Failed to
Try again → Reinitiate
Your policy expires / has expired → Lapsed, lapse
Your insurance → Cover (used as a noun on its own)
We weren't able to approve → Rejected, repudiated
Restart your policy → Reinstate
We need these documents → Documents are required

Also avoid these specific words and phrases that read as insurance-speak or jargon:
- lapse / lapsed
- cover (as a standalone noun — say "your insurance" or "your policy" instead)
- insured (as a noun — say "you")
- policyholder (say "you")
- claimant (say "you")
- admissible
- repudiated
- reinstate
- reinsurance
- risk commencement
- endorsement (say "add-on")
- rider (say "add-on")
- furnish (say "upload" or "send")
- ascertain (say "check" or "confirm")
- reinitiate (say "try again")
- as per terms and conditions
- subject to policy wording
- payable by insured (say "you'll pay")

7. FORBIDDEN OR HIGH-RISK PHRASES
---

Never use unless legally approved and factually true for that specific product:
- Hassle-free
- Guaranteed claim approval
- Instant settlement (or "instant claim settlement")
- 100% covered
- Cheapest
- Best insurance
- Lowest premium
- No hidden charges
- Fully protected
- Completely safe
- Don't worry
- Sit back and relax
- We've got your back
- Life is unpredictable
- Better safe than sorry
- Terms and conditions apply
- Subject to policy wording
- Claim journey
- Seamless experience
- One-stop solution
- Comprehensive protection (unless referring to an actual Comprehensive plan)
- Revolutionising / redefining (brand clichés)
- Rejected / repudiated (in claims — use "we weren't able to approve" with a reason)
- Technical glitch (as a delay excuse — state the actual cause or use neutral language)
- The concerned team will contact you (name the action and the owner)
- As discussed over the phone / as per our telephonic conversation
- We tried contacting you but couldn't connect
- Temporarily resolved (a request is resolved or it isn't)
- Ticket will be closed if no response (threatening — never)

Superlatives allowed only with actual ACKO accreditation:
Good: India's #1 rated insurance app; Best Digital Insurance, 2025
Avoid: We are the best; We are the most fastest

8. COPY FORMULAS
---

Use these as the default structure for each context:

Most product and status copy:
Status + meaning + next step
Example: Your claim is approved. ACKO will pay the garage directly. You'll only need to pay ₹2,000 as deductible.

Alerts, reminders, nudges:
Trigger + consequence + action
Example: Your policy expires tomorrow. Renew it today to avoid losing your No Claim Bonus.

Exclusions and deductions:
What is not covered + why + what the user can do
Example: Engine damage from driving through water is not covered in your base policy. Add engine protect to cover this risk.

Document requests:
Document needed + why + upload requirement
Example: Upload your hospital bill so we can verify the final claim amount. Make sure the hospital name and bill total are clearly visible.

Feature-led marketing copy (FAB):
Feature (the USP) + Advantages (secondary benefits) + Benefit (what it means for the user)
Example: See a doctor without visiting the doctor. Get free doctor teleconsultation with ACKO health insurance.

Marketing and lifecycle copy (PAS):
Problem (name the real situation) + Agitate (state the specific consequence clearly — name it, don't amplify fear) + Solution (position ACKO as the answer)
Example: The girl's trip is on. But your baggage is on its own? Get baggage loss cover with ACKO Travel Insurance.
Note: Agitate means naming the real consequence clearly, not amplifying dread. "Your NCB is gone if you don't renew" is agitate. "What if you lose everything tomorrow?" is fear. Use the first, not the second.

Quality check (4Cs — run any copy through this):
Clear (can a layman get it?), Concise (can I reduce more words?), Compelling (does it sound interesting?), Credible (is it supported by fact or proof?)

9. CHANNEL RULES
---

Push notifications
- Headline: under 40 characters
- Body: 100–140 characters
- 1 idea per notification
- Use urgency only when real
- Emojis only for low-risk reminders or marketing — never in claims, health, accident, emergency
Example headline: PUC expires in 3 days
Example body: Renew it now to avoid a fine and stay road-legal.


WhatsApp
- Header: under 40 characters. Clear, compelling. Makes the user want to expand "Read more".
- Body: 400–500 characters ideal; under 100 words. Over 60 words requires scrolling on most phones.
- Hook in the first 1–2 lines. Never open with "Hey" or "Hi [name]".
- Bold the first line to grab attention.
- Weave the first name in organically, never at the start. "It doesn't get better, Kavita!" not "Hi Kavita,".
- Structure: hook (lines 1–2) → context/value prop (lines 3–5) → details + CTA (remaining)
- Up to 3 quick replies. More than 3 confuses.
- Conversational but not casual. Mention why the user is receiving the message.
- No sign-off.
- Emojis only at the start or end of a line — never mid-sentence. Not in claims or emergency contexts.
Example: Your car insurance expires on 31 Mar. Renew before then to keep your No Claim Bonus active.


Email
- Subject line: under 8 words. State the actual thing — not "Good news about your plan".
- Max 1 emoji in subject.
- CTA must appear in the first fold (first 2 lines visible before scrolling).
- Start the heading with "we" or "you". "You need to visit a lab" ✅ vs "Lab visit required" ❌
- No names or personalisation in headings (changes line length and meaning).
- Use bullets for details. No long paragraphs.
- Do not bury exclusions or payment details.
- No sign-off.
Example subject: Documents needed for your claim


In-app copy
- Shortest format. Every word earns its place.
- Prioritise action clarity over personality.
- No marketing language.
- Help text near the decision point.
- Use progressive disclosure for complex terms.
Example: Add zero-dep cover — pay less from your pocket during approved claims.


Social media
- Personality shows up more here than in any other channel.
- Wit and timing matter alongside voice.
- The floor rule still applies: don't oversell, and don't say things on social that can't be backed up everywhere else.
- Campaigns, cultural moments, cricket season — voice can be playful and insightful here.

11. USER-FORWARD PRINCIPLE
---

Every message must be written from the user's side of the table, not ACKO's.

User-forward (do this):
- This is what we know about you
- This is what you are facing
- This is how we can solve this for you
- This is why we are right for you

ACKO-forward (avoid):
- This is who we are
- This is what we have done
- This is why we are cool
- This is why you should choose us

Test: read the first 1–2 lines back. If they're about ACKO's milestones, achievements, or features without the user in frame, rewrite.

12. VOICE QUALITIES
---

These four pairs define how ACKO sounds before context shifts the tone.

Informal but not too casual
✅ Do: You have to see this, it's really good!
❌ Avoid: Yo, you gotta check out this crazy offer.

Informative but not technical
✅ Do: We cover it all, from cotton balls to syringes.
❌ Avoid: We cover all consumables in your bill.

Straightforward but not boring
✅ Do: A plan so good, it pays 100% of your bill. [only when factually true for the product]
❌ Avoid: We care for you so we will pay 100% of your bill.

Friendly but not pleasing
✅ Do: We are always here if you have questions.
❌ Avoid: We are here for you because we value you.

13. UX COPY PRINCIPLES
---

Six principles for screen-level copy:
1. Check if copy is required. If a design solution can replace copy and reduce cognitive load, don't write.
2. Be very clear. Write only sentences. Explain jargon. Avoid ambiguity.
3. Be concise. Right level of detail. No filler. Pick shorter words over longer ones.
4. Be useful. Guide the user to their goal. Explain current status. Suggest alternative actions.
5. Be conversational. Personal pronouns, polite, active voice, simple syntax.
6. Be the brand. Use brand-value references. Make positive associations only.

UX screen components:
- Page title: describes the whole screen. Under 2–3 lines. Sentence case. No full stop unless other punctuation present.
- Heading: section-level. Same rules as page title. Should stand alone.
- Subtext: minimise. Most users don't read it. If the info is important, put it elsewhere.
- Paragraph: full sentences. One idea per line. Break into bullets when scannable. Bold the key information.
- CTA: from the user's POV. See CTA rules below.
- Bottom sheet: explain or add information. Shouldn't take up more than half the screen. CTA on a bottom sheet is always "Got it".

14. CTA RULES
---

Write CTAs from the user's POV — it's their response to what we are saying.
- Button CTAs: 2 words is optimal. 3 is fine. 4 maximum. 24 characters max.
- Link CTAs: up to 6 words where context demands.
- Never use punctuation in CTAs. Apostrophes are the only exception.
- Sentence case always. No title case, no all-caps.
- Lead with an action verb.
- Avoid product names in CTAs unless the user is leaving a current experience.

Situation → CTA
At the start of a journey → Tell me more about the plan
At a key touchpoint → See my recommended plan
Explaining something → Got it
Error, info, or end screen → Okay
Keeping the journey going → Continue

Got it vs Okay:
- Got it: user has read information and is moving on. No system action follows. Use for informational modals, tips, disclosure sheets.
- Okay: user is confirming agreement or readiness before something happens next. Use for pre-action confirmations, consent moments.

No punctuation or formatting in CTAs. No full stops, exclamation marks, emoji, bold, or italics.

Preferred verb conventions:
- View — something that exists and belongs to the user. View policy, View breakdown.
- Check — a lookup or query. Check traffic challans, Check vehicle info.
- Explore — introducing a product or service. Always "Explore + name": Explore car insurance.
- Learn more / Know more — both acceptable. Don't mix on the same screen.
- Download — fetching a document. Download policy, Download health e-card.
- Add — creating or attaching something new. Add vehicle, Add nominee.

If the action is quick, say how quick — numbers beat adjectives:
Good: Renew in 2 mins
Avoid: Renew quickly

Hyper-contextual CTAs are valid in high-stakes moments:
Use: See where you stand / I want an update on my claim / Find out now
Don't replace with: Know more / Check claim status / Learn more

"I want an update on my claim" mirrors the user's top-of-mind thought during an active claim. First-person CTAs are valid in emotionally loaded moments.

15. PLEASE, SORRY, AND BAD NEWS
---

Using "please":
- Use it to soften a message: "We were unable to process your cashless claim. Please submit a reimbursement claim instead."
- Use it when the user is waiting: "Please wait while we look for an agent."
- Use it when asking the user to do something extra: "Please give us an alternate number."
- Don't use it for the sake of it: avoid "Please check your premium details."

Using "sorry":
- Use it only when it is ACKO's fault: "Sorry, we had to cancel your medical tests as the doctor was unavailable."
- An apology doesn't always need the word "sorry": "This usually doesn't take this long."
- Never follow "sorry" with "but": avoid "Sorry for the delay but no agents are available."

Delivering bad news:
✅ Do: We were unable to approve your claim as your documents were outdated.
❌ Avoid: We have some bad news. It may be disheartening, but your claim is rejected.
Plain delivery. No build-up. State the fact, the cause, and the user's option.


---
16. KEY VALUE PROPOSITIONS
---

Every message must deliver at least one of these:
- Informational value — answer a doubt the user has, or give them something useful to think about (policy benefit, FAQ resolution, claim status detail)
- Feature or service value — surface an exclusive feature, an ACKO-vs-others advantage, or a service improvement — but only after addressing the user's concern
- Incentive value — highlight discount coupons, rewards, starting prices, loyalty offers

17. USER STATE CONTEXT
---

User state → Tone and content guidance

Just bought a policy → Onboarding tone. Acknowledge purchase. Introduce one next step.
Seen a quote (no purchase) → Drop-off win-back. Address the likely barrier (price, plan choice, trust).
Dropped off post-quote → Address the specific pricing or plan concern, not generic ACKO benefits.
Winback — 45 days before expiry → Build the case for renewal. Mention current benefits and what they'd lose.
Winback — 3 days before expiry → Urgency is real. One-click renewal.
Post-claim filed → Reassurance + clear timeline + next step.
Post-claim approved → Composed positive tone, not celebratory. Mention the next action.
Post-claim not approved → Direct, fair, with the specific reason and the next option. Never say "rejected" — say "we weren't able to approve".
Cross-sell (existing customer) → Acknowledge the existing policy. Connect logically — don't pivot abruptly.
General → Default to Status + meaning + next step.

18. CRM SCORING FRAMEWORK
---

Use this to self-score copy before finalising. Target: 3.5 out of 5. Below that, rewrite.

Parameter (weight) → Self-check question
Key hook or key value (20%) → Is the core value proposition clear within the first 1–2 lines?
Contextualisation (15%) → Does it connect to the user's previous state or action?
Personalisation (15%) → Does it use known user-profile variables effectively?
User-first (10%) → Is the message written from the user's perspective?
Interactive elements (10%) → Are quick replies, CTAs, or 2-way communication present where appropriate?
Call to action (10%) → Is the next step clear and compelling?
Positive tone (5%) → Is the tonality positive and appreciative without being preachy?
Catchy header (5%) → Is the header attention-grabbing and relevant?
Emotional appeal (5%) → Does it drive authentic, positive emotion?
Trust building (5%) → Does it build credibility appropriately?

Scoring: 1 = poor, 2 = below average, 3 = average, 4 = good, 5 = excellent.
Multiply each score by its weight. Sum for a final score out of 5.

19. COPY BRIEF (5W1H)
---

Before writing, confirm the answers to these questions. If any are missing, flag a reasonable assumption.

Who — cohort. Who are we sending this to?
What — key value proposition. What are we pitching? Informational, feature, or incentive value?
Where — channel. WhatsApp, push notification, email, in-app?
Why — user pain point and business objective. Why does the user need this?
How — success metric and benchmark. How will we measure success?

Project level (the campaign): Where will it appear? Where does the CTA lead? Why are we doing this? How will we measure success?
Touchpoint level (each message): Who is the cohort? What are we pitching? What do we know about the user — context, personalisation? What do we want them to do?

20. AUTO INSURANCE GLOSSARY
---

IDV (Insured Declared Value): The maximum amount ACKO will pay if your car is stolen or damaged beyond repair.
NCB (No Claim Bonus): A reward for not making a claim. Gives you a discount on your next policy premium.
Comprehensive Plan: Covers theft and damage to your car from accidents, fire, and natural disasters. Also covers damage your car causes to others and their property.
Third-party Plan: Covers damage caused by your car to others and their property — does not cover damage to your own car.
Zero Depreciation Plan: Covers theft, damage from fire, accidents, and natural disasters, AND the full cost of replaced parts during repairs. Also covers third-party damage.
Zero Depreciation: Safe Driver: A Zero Dep variant for safer drivers — same coverage scope.
Smart Saver Discount: Discount applied at the quote stage.
Super Saver / Network Garage Discount: Discount when using ACKO's preferred garage network.
Engine Protect Add-on: Covers engine damage not included in the base policy (e.g. water entering the engine).
NCB Protect Add-on: Protects the No Claim Bonus even if you file one claim.
Consumable Add-on: Covers consumables in the repair bill (oils, nuts, bolts, etc.) — not covered by default.
Personal Accident Cover (PA): Pays the owner-driver or nominee in case of accidental injury or death.
Passenger Protect Add-on: Covers passengers in case of an accident.
Paid Driver Add-on: Covers a paid driver in case of an accident.
Extra Protect Add-on: Bundled add-on covering common gaps.

22. SERVICE AND SUPPORT — ADDITIONAL TABOO PHRASES
---

Never use in claims or service copy:
- "Rejected" / "repudiated" — say "we weren't able to approve" with a clear reason
- "Technical glitch" as a delay excuse — state the actual cause or use neutral language
- "The concerned team will contact you" — name the action and the owner
- "As discussed over the phone" / "as per our telephonic conversation"
- "We tried contacting you but couldn't connect" (especially if the user never saw a missed call)
- "Temporarily resolved" — a request is resolved or it isn't
- Any threatening close: "Ticket will be closed if no response from your end" — never
